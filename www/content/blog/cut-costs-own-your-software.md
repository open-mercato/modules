_Field notes from two weeks of meetings in New York and San Francisco._

I spent the first half of November in New York and San Francisco. I met founders, agency owners, CTOs and people who work with private equity portfolios.

I expected to talk about AI agents. We mostly talked about costs.

## The mood has changed

For years the easy pitch in software was revenue. Buy this tool and you will sell more.

That pitch is harder today. The market moves too fast for a three-year forecast. As an angel investor I see startups reach $1M ARR in a month. Some of them shut down a few weeks later.

Owners of mid-size companies say the same thing in different words. They have no idea what comes next. Cutting costs and lifting EBITDA never hurts.

One services executive told me about a call from his biggest client. Same team, 20% lower price, or the headcount goes down. That call is happening in a lot of companies right now.

There is a second effect. Most software vendors still sell growth. Very few sell a smaller bill. So the cost side has a lot of empty space.

## You pay for 100%. You use 5%.

My favourite question in these meetings is simple. How much do you pay for software licenses every year?

One answer I keep hearing is about $500k for a single CRM suite. Then I ask how much of it the team uses. The usual answer is 'almost nothing, it is too complex'.

So the offer is short. Build your 5% on Open Mercato. [Own the app](/blog-posts/open-mercato-license-you-keep-the-code). Stop renting the other 95%.

Open Mercato is an open-source foundation for business applications. Customers, sales, catalog, warehouse, permissions and audit are already there. Your coding agents build the part that is specific to your company. They work inside our guardrails, so the result is maintainable.

**You control the cost in three places:**

- **License.** We price per project, never per seat. Hiring 50 people does not change your bill.
- **Code.** The app is yours. You decide when to upgrade and what to change.
- **Models.** You can build with open models. Rising token prices become your choice.

Compare that with a story from one of our earlier companies. A client connected a small integration to their ERP. The vendor counted every connection as a named user. The client had 500 users on paper. The vendor claimed 10,000 and asked for a new license calculation.

That is what a rented cost base looks like. Someone else holds the calculator.

## The apps are boring. Good.

We checked what people build on Open Mercato. Every single app is boring.

One example. A distributor sells syringes and medical gloves by the million. Every sales conversation starts with the price of gloves. Win the gloves with custom pricing and the whole catalog follows. They needed a small quoting app built around that one rule.

Other teams replace a CRM suite. Others add new apps next to an ERP they no longer want to extend.

Our typical customer has $100M to $1B in revenue. Manufacturers, clinic chains, distributors. The owner is often still in the building. The owner says: use AI, cut the costs, show me the number.

## Why PE funds like this approach

PE funds in the US already send us their portfolio companies. When they hear 'you can remove the CRM suite', the reaction is one word. Great.

I see three reasons.

**1. A cost cut is certain.** A license bill is a line in the P\&L. Remove it and the next quarter shows it. Now the simple math. Save $400k a year and apply a 10x EBITDA multiple. That is $4M of enterprise value from one decision. Use your own multiple.

**2. The clock is short.** A fund holds a company for a few years. A system rollout that takes 18 months eats a big part of that. Our partners deliver new projects 3-4 times faster on Open Mercato. One agency we work with kept its price and cut delivery time by four. Their clients are happy, because the budget was already approved.

**3. The playbook repeats.** One investor asked me about carve-outs. A carved-out business needs its own systems almost from zero. Speed decides there. Every buy-and-build portfolio has a version of this.

People who run tech due diligence for PE buyers gave me one more lesson. They sell a measurable EBITDA improvement. No board asks for more lines of code.

## What I would not rebuild

Do not rebuild everything. That would be a stupid way to spend engineering time.

Invoicing for $200 a month? Buy it. The law changes, someone else updates it, and a mistake there costs more than the license. Simple utility SaaS will stay. Your agents will call it through an API like any other infrastructure.

The exposed products are the big suites. You can configure everything in them and nobody understands the result.

Second warning. A working prototype is still far from an application. Production needs permissions, audit, tests, upgrades and someone responsible for all of it.

Third warning. Start with a few workflows around the ERP. Leave the full replacement for later, after a proper audit.
