## A finished product should not lose its history

A customer order describes what a manufacturer needs to ship. A recipe describes what needs to go into it. A warehouse record says which materials are available. The difficult part is keeping those records connected when real production starts.

For food and supplement manufacturing, a finished batch also needs a history: which supplier LOTs went into it, how materials were consumed and where the resulting product went. When those connections live across spreadsheets and separate documents, answering a question about a batch becomes an exercise in reconstruction.

TraceCore grew out of BGW Pharma’s own production of premium supplements. The team built and tested the system in its manufacturing operation before rewriting it on Open Mercato for the wider market. The starting point was a working factory and its operational needs.

## Connect the order to the work that fulfils it

TraceCore brings customer orders, quotes, pricing and tax into the same system as production planning. That gives the commercial side of the business a shared foundation with the people making and moving the product.

Production planning combines recipes, tasks and material consumption. Warehouse management covers locations, stock, expiration dates and inventory audits. Supplier and carrier records extend the workflow to incoming materials and shipment documentation.

The value is in the connection between those areas. An order is part of an operational chain involving a recipe, available materials, production work and a shipment. Keeping each part in the same system gives the team a place to follow that chain.

## A recipe has to survive contact with production

The recipe module carries the definition of what the team intends to produce. Planning and material-consumption records connect that definition to the work carried out on the floor.

That distinction matters whenever the business needs to explain a particular batch. Knowing the standard recipe is one piece of the story; knowing the materials and LOTs involved in actual production is another. TraceCore’s production and warehouse workflows are built around keeping both available.

Rather than treating stock, recipes and tasks as unrelated lists, the system brings them into the same operational context. Operators, production managers, warehouse staff and sales teams work with different parts of that context.

## Make the LOT connection part of the system

TraceCore includes native LOT traceability and audit trails. The purpose is to preserve the links between incoming materials, production and the finished goods that leave the operation.

A batch question then has a starting point in the application. The team can inspect the recorded traceability relationships rather than rely on someone remembering which document contains the next link.

The traceability view makes those relationships visible. Alongside the underlying audit trail, it supports the practical work of investigating production history and preparing documentation.

## Keep the manufacturing model, reuse the application foundation

Open Mercato supplies the application core on which TraceCore’s manufacturing modules are built. Its MIT-licensed foundation gives the team an existing structure to extend while focusing on recipes, production, warehouse operations and traceability.

TraceCore supports deployment on premises or on a dedicated VPS. For a manufacturer, this makes the location and operation of its business data part of the implementation choice.

The product therefore combines a reusable software foundation with a domain model shaped by a manufacturer’s own work. The specialised value is the way the operational records fit together across the factory.

## From an internal build to a repeatable implementation

Having started in BGW Pharma’s own production, TraceCore is now offered to other manufacturers. Its published implementation model targets the first LOT in the system by day five and a broader rollout over twelve weeks.
