## The question that outlasts the email thread

A client asks why their licensing agreement contains a particular clause. The lawyer who negotiated it six months ago remembers the conversation. What happens when a colleague takes the call?

[Creativa Legal](https://creativa.legal/) promises clients a lead lawyer who knows their business. Much of that knowledge lives in individual inboxes. Pipedrive holds the sales history, ClickUp holds the tasks, and meeting transcripts hold pieces of the reasoning. The firm connected its tools with automations. A lawyer picking up a file still had to piece together the story.

Creativa Legal’s business model already called for one place to look up a client’s history. How to build it was an open question. In July 2026 the firm’s managing partner, Arkadiusz Szczudło, started building CreativaOS in-house on Open Mercato. He worked with AI coding agents and consulted specialists without hiring a software house.

## Open the client file where the conversation left off

In the Northwind Studio example, a contact asks whether the license term can start on the signing date. Her email about §7 appears on the client’s timeline. Scroll down and there’s a meeting, a colleague’s note about a new request, and an earlier version of the agreement. The lawyer following up has somewhere to start even if the original exchange never reached their inbox.

The same history matters when the handover happens on the client’s side. A new contact may be meeting the firm for the first time. Creativa Legal wants the lawyer to be able to show what their predecessor agreed and explain how they got there.

## From a booked consultation to time on the file

A prospective client can book a free initial consultation lasting 15 to 30 minutes. The booking appears in the lawyer’s calendar. In the example client file, the appointment also appears as an entry on the timeline, alongside the correspondence that follows.

<figure><img src="/media/case-studies/6b055baf2edeb6ac37618fa8-6b055976a1eb379e08798a47-creativaos-bookings.jpeg" alt="Creativa Legal’s consultation booking page showing available times for a 30-minute appointment." /><figcaption>The visitor sees available slots for an initial consultation. The lawyer’s other appointments stay off the booking page.</figcaption></figure>

For the lawyer, the day starts with deadlines, meetings and new mail. An email shows which client it belongs to. On the time screen, the lawyer can run a timer against work as specific as reviewing a licensing agreement, then see that entry in the client’s row for the week.

## What Creativa Legal built on Open Mercato

By Creativa Legal’s own count, the team can see 302 features. The firm estimates that 4% use Open Mercato as supplied, 23% adapt an existing feature, and 73% were built from scratch. Those figures describe what the team sees on screen. The custom features run on the Open Mercato core too. Two of the firm’s additions are the Microsoft 365 integration and an MCP server for Claude.

One reason for building so much in-house is how the firm assigns work. Creativa Legal already matches new clients to lawyers by industry. Its sales tools can only draw on what someone put in Pipedrive. The firm wants whoever makes the next assignment to have the history of the work as well.

## What the lawyer sees before anything goes to a client

A client may send over a contract draft written with an AI chatbot. The harder question is whether that draft fits what the parties agreed last time. For now, Creativa Legal reviews contracts with separate AI tools outside CreativaOS. The firm is designing its assistant to read the same client file, notes and tasks a lawyer would consult. A lawyer would review each result and handle all communication with the client.

At the time of writing, Creativa Legal plans to roll out the workspace by the end of 2026. Invoicing is a later addition. The test the team cares about is a handover: can the next lawyer explain a decision to the client without first tracking down the person who made it?
